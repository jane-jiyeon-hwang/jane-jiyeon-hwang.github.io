Analysis of the service area of COVID-19 testing medical institutions by epidemic period

Jiyeon Hwang Jiwoo Seo Minsu Kim Kyumin Seo
2022 Annual Conference of the Korean Geographical Society
*

Abstract

Regional disparities between the Seoul Metropolitan Area and non-metropolitan regions have long been a persistent issue in South Korea. As the country underwent rapid industrialization and economic development, growth became increasingly concentrated in the metropolitan area centered on Seoul, resulting in uneven regional development. In the healthcare sector, disparities in both the availability and accessibility of preventive medical service facilities have emerged as key indicators of regional inequality.

With the outbreak of COVID-19, the Korean government established COVID-19 testing facilities across the country. These facilities, located either outside emergency rooms or within medically separated areas of healthcare institutions, were designed to allow individuals with suspected symptoms to receive testing before entering general medical spaces. While the expansion of testing facilities contributed to the early detection of confirmed cases, accessibility to these facilities became a critical component of regional disease control as the pandemic intensified and testing demand increased.

This study divides the COVID-19 pandemic into five distinct phases and applies network-based analysis to examine the service areas of COVID-19 testing facilities alongside the number of confirmed cases per 10,000 residents at the provincial level. By analyzing spatial and temporal variations in testing accessibility, this research aims to provide practical evidence for developing public health service support strategies that address and reduce quantitative regional disparities during infectious disease outbreaks.
