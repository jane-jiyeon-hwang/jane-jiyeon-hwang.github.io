Evaluating Urban Environmental Sustainability Using SDG indicators: A Case Study of Paju, South Korea

Abstract

This study evaluates urban environmental sustainability in Paju, Gyeonggi Province, South Korea, based on Goal 11 of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): Sustainable Cities and Communities. Focusing on the Korean SDG (K-SDG) indicators related to housing (11.1) and transportation (11.2), spatial analysis was conducted at the eup-myeon-dong administrative level. The analysis utilized data such as the proportion of aged housing, the supply of public rental housing, bus stop density, and the distribution rate of low-floor buses. In addition, qualitative field investigations were carried out to capture aspects not reflected in quantitative data. Based on these findings, this study highlights the limitations of the current K-SDG indicators and argues for the introduction of complementary qualitative indicators.

In the housing sector, residential conditions across Paju were assessed using the indicators of aged housing ratio and public rental housing supply. The spatial analysis revealed that southern Paju, including Unjeong New Town, exhibited relatively low proportions of aged housing and higher levels of rental housing supply, whereas northern Paju areas such as Beobwon-eup and Papyeong-myeon showed high concentrations of aged housing and elevated risks of population decline. To supplement these findings, field observations and interviews with residents and urban regeneration center staff were conducted in Geumchon-dong and Beobwon-eup. In Geumchon-dong, certain neighborhoods—such as the Saemal district—contained dense clusters of deteriorated housing that were not adequately captured in official statistics, exposing limitations in the existing indicators. Conversely, although Beobwon-eup recorded a high aged housing ratio, active housing improvements through the Saetteul Village Project were underway, and residents reported high levels of satisfaction, revealing a discrepancy between quantitative indicators and on-the-ground conditions.

In the transportation sector, public transit accessibility was evaluated using bus stop density and low-floor bus distribution rates. To assess the actual accessibility experienced by transportation-vulnerable groups, on-site surveys of bus stops in Geumchon-dong and Beobwon-eup were conducted, including direct measurements of curb heights. The results showed that many bus stops in Paju failed to meet the 15 cm curb height standard required for low-floor bus ramps, making wheelchair boarding difficult. In addition, tactile paving and audio guidance systems were largely absent. In-depth interviews with older adults and persons with disabilities further revealed that, although many were aware of low-floor buses, they often chose not to use them due to factors such as pressure from drivers, social stigma, and lack of information. These findings suggest that simply expanding the number of low-floor buses is insufficient to achieve meaningful improvements in accessibility.

Based on these results, this study proposes the introduction of additional qualitative indicators to complement the current K-SDG framework, including the availability of special transportation services (e.g., mobility support taxis for persons with disabilities), the provision of welfare-oriented transport services (e.g., low-cost community taxis), and the physical accessibility of bus stops (e.g., curb height, tactile paving, and audio guidance systems).
